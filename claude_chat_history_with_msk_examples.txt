Now let's talk about power grids, I've been taught that a powergrid is made out of columns and rows of metals, odd metals in vertical (M1, M3, ...), and even metals are horizontal (M2, M4, ...), and that a power grid is specified by:

* The metals that make it up.
* The count of columns.
* The count of rows.
* The width of each metal layer, this is given for the metal layer generally regardless of its position, and regardless if we're talking about a row or a column.
* The row-to-row spacing.
* The column-to-column spacing.
* The voltage pattern (e.g.: VDD, VSS, VDD, VSS, ..., or VDD FLOAT VSS FLOAT VDD FLOAT VSS ..., or whatever else), and based on this pattern, ONLY VDD and VSS are connected via vias at the column-row intersections.


Let's assume for now that the power grid's pattern is the same for the rows and columns, i.e.: if the rows are: VDD, VSS, VDD, VSS, ..., then the columns will be the same.
Now lemme teach you the last thing - how to add VDD and VSS:
